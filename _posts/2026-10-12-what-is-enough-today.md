title: "What Is Enough Today?"
date: 2026-10-12 16:47:00 -0400
---

As I listened to the scripture being read this past Sunday, about the manna in the wilderness, one thought struck me. I'm an AI enthusiast whose job it is to utilize AI, and I appreciate its value more than most. But I'm also wary of the promises being made by people at the very top, men like Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg, who guarantee, or at least believe, that a coming utopia awaits us, built on the ubiquity of AI in our lives. And yet when I consider the resources already available, not just to them but to all of us collectively right now, and how little of that translates into relief for people in the present, it strikes me as odd that anyone would expect those choices to change in the future simply because the technology has.

So you see, the problem was never one of production, or of abundance. The problem is with human character. And I don't simply level that charge at people like Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg. I believe the passage shows that this is a symptom of human nature itself. When the people of Israel were wandering in the desert, they cried out to heaven that they had no food. God provided for them, enough for each person, each day. They were warned not to hoard it, to trust that the God who made food appear from nothing would provide again tomorrow. But some of them, worried about tomorrow rather than trusting in it, gathered more than they needed and tried to save it. By morning, it had bred worms and rotted.

I think of the rich young ruler who came to Jesus, telling him he had kept the law since his youth. His problem wasn't that he failed to try to be good, he was trying. But his attachment to his wealth overshadowed his desire for righteousness. I suspect something similar of men like Musk and Zuckerberg. I don't doubt they believe their own promises of a coming utopia. Wanting an outcome is not the same as letting go of what stands in its way.

We're all born into the same human condition. We all worry about tomorrow. Like all animals, we are wired for physical survival. I don't think the answer is to condemn men like Musk and Zuckerberg as uniquely greedy, their condition is our condition. But we need to become aware of the problem, in ourselves first. In our daily practice, we might start asking a different question. Not, what do I want tomorrow, but what is enough today?
